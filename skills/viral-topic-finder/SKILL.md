# Viral Topic Finder

Generate specific, filmable ideas that people already care about. Combine audience research with the D.R.N.B.R. selection method. "Viral" describes the ambition, not a guaranteed outcome.

## Personalize on first use

Read [personalization.md](references/personalization.md). Reuse an established
profile or ask a brief discovery question about niche, audience, goal, platforms,
and boundaries. Optional examples help; personal identity is unnecessary. Let the
user skip optional choices, and do not repeat discovery when context is sufficient.
Save a profile only when agreed, outside the installed skill.

## Establish the brief

Use the current conversation and supplied materials to identify the niche, target viewer, content goal, requested count, and any constraints. Default to 25 ideas for short-form video. Honor a different count, format, audience, or research scope.

If the niche or audience is missing and cannot be inferred, ask one concise question before researching. Infer optional details and state material assumptions briefly. Do not force an ecommerce or AI angle onto an unrelated niche. Use previously supplied content or audience data when relevant and available; compare with earlier topics when the user asks for new ideas.

Keep each idea small enough for one short video: one central insight, a visible example, and one useful takeaway. Do not impose a fixed runtime unless requested.

## Research demand before selecting topics

Understand who does the work, who makes decisions, how value is created, major costs, repetitive tasks, expensive mistakes, customer questions, and recent changes. For a nonbusiness niche, use the audience's equivalent goals, frustrations, and decisions.

Use available search and browsing tools to find repeated problems, questions, beliefs, and behaviors. Useful sources include communities, Reddit, reviews, customer complaints, video comments, search results, industry discussions, case studies, surveys, and competitor content. Look for language such as "How do I...", "We keep having...", "This stopped working...", and "Customers keep...". Follow relevant leads until the major topic territories have concrete support.

- Prefer behavioral evidence: repeated questions, recurring complaints, spending, and attempts to solve a problem. A popular post is a signal, not proof of a widespread need.
- Cross-check themes across independent sources where possible. Search results alone do not establish search volume or current platform popularity. Do not invent metrics or imply access to private comments or analytics.
- For changing technology, AI, software, platforms, regulations, algorithms, and market conditions, verify current claims. Check publication and event dates. Prioritize recent sources for timely topics, while retaining useful older evidence for evergreen problems.
- Use audience discussions to establish demand. Use primary sources to verify technical claims, product changes, statistics, and documented outcomes. An announcement proves a feature exists, not that the audience wants it.
- Keep source links connected to the ideas they support. Distinguish verified facts, audience signals, interpretations, predictions, and hypothetical illustrations.
- If browsing is unavailable or the user asks for no research, use supplied evidence and disclose that current demand is unverified. Label unsupported ideas exploratory. Do not describe them as proven.

For news, identify what changed, why it matters to this viewer, and what they should do next. A launch announcement without a useful consequence is not yet a topic.

## Generate, score, and refine

Read [the scoring rubric](references/scoring-rubric.md) before selecting the final list.

1. Privately generate at least twice the requested number of candidates. For the default 25, generate at least 50. Avoid returning the first batch unchanged.
2. Make candidates specific tensions, mistakes, questions, observations, or opportunities. "AI for ecommerce" is a subject. "Your support tickets reveal the questions your product page never answers" is a topic.
3. Remove collisions at the insight level. Ask: "If I removed the hooks, are these the same idea?" The same data source can support different topics when the problem, decision, or lesson differs.
4. Evaluate every remaining candidate on Demand, Reach, Novelty, Business Value, and Repeatability, each from 1 to 5. Identify weak dimensions. Rewrite promising ideas with weak packaging, then reevaluate them. Discard those that still fail.
5. Select the strongest diverse portfolio. Balance broad reach, core authority, tactical advice, contrarian insight, timely changes, and evergreen problems as appropriate. Do not force equal quotas or pick only one high-scoring territory.
6. Replenish the candidate pool if deduplication or rejection leaves too few worthwhile ideas. Deliver the requested count without padding. If limited evidence prevents a fully supported list, clearly label exploratory entries and explain the evidence limit briefly.

Keep the candidate pool, numeric scores, and rejected ideas out of the answer unless requested. When asked for scoring, provide concise ratings and evidence-based justifications.

## Package each idea for the camera

Favor core, casual, and new audience fit: the core audience recognizes the problem, adjacent viewers understand the situation, and a new viewer understands the stakes without technical background. Reach is about comprehension and relevance, not just market size.

Apply these checks:

- **Two-second hook:** A scrolling viewer understands the tension without reading the explanation. The video must deliver the hook's promise.
- **Camera test:** Identify a moment that could be filmed or screenshotted. Replace "inefficient workflows" with "an employee copies the same order into a spreadsheet every Friday".
- **So what test:** Connect the observation to a meaningful consequence and a specific action the viewer can take today.
- **Novel but familiar:** Use a fresh explanation of a recognizable problem. Avoid generic tool lists, "AI will change everything", "work smarter, not harder", and manufactured controversy.
- **Repeatability:** The insight can survive a new example or format, such as a demo, breakdown, case study, experiment, or reaction.

For AI topics, start with the problem. AI can be the mechanism. Show the practical chain when useful: input -> AI task -> decision or action -> result. Do not imply a workflow is reliable or available without checking relevant claims.

Write two hooks for the same idea using different hook types. Rotate types across the portfolio:

| Type | Approach |
| --- | --- |
| Contrarian | Challenge a common assumption with a defensible insight. |
| Framework | Introduce a simple rule the explanation actually teaches. |
| Labeling | Connect a behavior to an outcome, without insults. |
| Story lead-in | Open inside a concrete scene. Use the speaker's real story only when supplied. |
| Authority | Use a verified source, documented example, or credible expertise. |
| Curiosity gap | Create a clear information gap that the lesson resolves. |

Never invent personal experience, credentials, statistics, testimonials, or results for the user. Mark imagined scenarios as hypothetical. A vivid illustration is not evidence. Do not add unsupported numbers to make hooks sound precise.

Use plain spoken language at roughly a seventh-grade reading level. Keep sentences concrete and concise, with clear cause and effect. Avoid jargon, excessive adjectives, em dashes, and exaggerated performance promises. Prefer straight quotes.

## Deliver the ideas

Unless the user requests another format, provide exactly the requested number in this compact structure. Start with a brief audience or scope assumption only when needed.

### 1. [Short topic name]

**Question:** The real audience question this video answers.

**Hook:** A standalone opening line.

**Explain:** One or two sentences about the underlying mechanism.

**Illustrate:** A concrete, filmable moment. Label a hypothetical example as such.

**Lesson:** A specific action the viewer can take today. Use a short list only if multiple actions are needed.

**Hook 2:** A different hook type for the same insight.

**Evidence:** A short demand signal with a descriptive source link. Add a separate fact-check link when a factual claim needs different support. If evidence is unavailable or weak, say "Exploratory" and identify what needs verification.

Repeat for every topic. Keep citations in the evidence line, outside the spoken hooks. Avoid a long methodology introduction. Research and progress updates do not replace the requested ideas.

On follow-up, preserve the chosen insight while adapting the format. For a full recording script, use an available script-writing skill such as `$video-script` when it fits, or draft directly if none is available. This skill must work without that dependency. Publishing, filming, editing footage, and scheduling are separate requests.
