# Fictional worked example: A draft that is easier to review

This is a constructed demonstration, not a real creator's story, transcript, or
performance result. Do not present it as the user's experience.

## Brief and discovery

Raw notes: "Clients say the draft is wrong, but they don't say what is wrong.
Maybe ask better questions. Don't make this sound like their fault."

Example profile: freelance designers; warm, practical voice; short vertical video;
goal is useful comments; no personal anecdotes or sales offer supplied.

Refined thesis: separate feedback on the goal from feedback on execution.
Chosen hook: "If 'it doesn't feel right' is the only feedback you get, try changing the question."

## Example spoken copy

If "it doesn't feel right" is the only feedback you get, try changing the question.

A finished draft asks someone to judge a lot at once. The message, the layout,
the colors. They might know something feels off without knowing which part it is.

Before you ask whether they like it, ask what they think it is trying to do.

Who is this for? What is the main point? What should someone do next?

If those answers don't match the brief, fix the direction first. If they do,
you can talk about the details without guessing at the whole problem.

What question gets you the most useful feedback?
